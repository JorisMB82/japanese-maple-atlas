# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

RHS and Missouri Botanical Garden use ‘Katsura’; RHS marks the name accepted; Kew supports species placement only. No cultivar synonym is supplied by the reviewed institutional sources. [SRC-RC-009-001–003]

The unrelated katsura tree, *Cercidiphyllum japonicum*, is explicitly excluded. Missouri Botanical Garden presents the cultivar and species as separate records. [SRC-RC-009-006]

Traceable vegetatively propagated material, labelled but unauthenticated material, seedlings, similar spring-colour cultivars and the unrelated species remain separate identity states.

**Authentication limit:** spring colour and one label or photograph do not certify clonal identity.

---

# 3. Naming variants and synonyms

The accepted working name is *Acer palmatum* ‘Katsura’. No cultivar synonym is approved. Case, quotation and typographic variants do not create separate identities.

“Katsura” is also the English common name for *Cercidiphyllum japonicum*. Identity-sensitive contexts must use the full botanical combination, and cross-taxon search results must not populate this cultivar’s history or morphology.

No primary naming statement or reliable translation is approved. Geographic or linguistic inferences from the epithet remain excluded.

---

# 4. Botanical description

RHS and Missouri Botanical Garden converge on a compact upright cultivar with small palmate foliage whose strongest diagnostic expression is spring emergence: chartreuse/yellow-orange tissue with orange or pink margins, becoming green in summer. [SRC-RC-009-001–002]

**G4 synthesis:** ‘Katsura’ is governed as a seasonal trajectory rather than one static colour.

Quantitative morphology remains source-bounded where accession and measurement protocols are absent.

---

# 5. Whole-plant architecture

RHS describes an upright shrub; Missouri Botanical Garden describes dense compact growth, vase-shaped when young and more rounded with age. [SRC-RC-009-001–002]

Dimensions are age- and context-specific; they do not establish one fixed crown ratio or maximum. Architecture remains separate from leaf morphology and seasonal colour.

---

# 6. Leaf morphology

RHS reports five to seven lobes; Missouri Botanical Garden reports small five-lobed leaves with lanceolate lobes, slender tips and shallow teeth. The difference is retained rather than forced into one universal count. [SRC-RC-009-001–002]

Spring colour and margin pattern are descriptive but not independently authenticating. Standardized authenticated multi-accession measurements remain unavailable.

---

# 7. Seasonal characteristics

The governed sequence is yellow-orange/chartreuse spring emergence with orange or pink margins, bright-green summer foliage and orange/yellow autumn colour. Exact timing and hue vary. [SRC-RC-009-001–002, SRC-RC-009-004–005]

Early leaf-out may expose foliage to late frost; this affects display and is not identity failure. Seasonal colours are trajectories, not fixed values.

---

# 8. Horticultural information

Full sun to partial shade, moist freely draining soil and protection from extreme heat or wind are supported. Afternoon shade is prudent in hot climates; high-pH chlorosis and scorch remain contextual Japanese-maple risks. [SRC-RC-009-001–002]

RHS H6 and USDA 5–9 remain separate source frameworks. Species-level guidance is not silently promoted to cultivar-specific tolerance.

---

# 9. Historical provenance

RHS herbarium specimens from 1995, 1997 and 2000 and JC Raulston photograph records from 2018 document modern institutional use. They do not establish origin or clonal authentication. [SRC-RC-009-004–005]

Original selector, date, naming rationale and earliest introduction remain explicitly unresolved.

---

# 10. Propagation and cultivar continuity

RHS publishes grafting or softwood cuttings; vegetative propagation is required for continuity.

Seedlings do not inherit the named cultivar identity. Propagation does not authenticate incorrectly identified source material.

---

# 11. Diagnostic framework

Useful diagnosis combines timing, spring colour, marginal colour and compact upright architecture. Summer-only material has limited diagnostic value.

Key comparisons are RC-006 for autumn-led versus spring-led value, RC-005 for sustained yellow foliage versus transient spring colour, and RC-007 for structural variegation versus seasonal margins.

**Descriptive confidence:** high.  
**Clonal authentication from appearance alone:** low.
