# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

RHS accepts the hyphenated ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’; NC State uses spaced lowercase; JC Raulston and archived Chicago use title case. These are institutional display/search variants, not separate cultivar identities. No synonym is approved. [SRC-RC-010-001–004]

Kew supports *Acer palmatum* placement only. Source-reported name meaning and commercial Tōkai/Mikawa-origin stories are not converted into primary provenance. [SRC-RC-010-002, SRC-RC-010-007]

Traceable vegetatively propagated material, labelled but unauthenticated material, seedlings, other yatsubusa-type selections and plants made to appear layered by pruning or angle remain separate identity states.

**Authentication limit:** dwarf scale and layered foliage are distinctive together but do not authenticate an individual plant.

---

# 3. Naming variants and synonyms

The accepted working form is ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’. ‘Mikawa yatsubusa’ and ‘Mikawa Yatsubusa’ are source/search variants. No cultivar synonym is approved.

NC State’s name meaning is retained only as a source-reported interpretation. Commercial claims of a specific former Mikawa/Tōkai wild origin or 1970s discovery remain excluded pending primary evidence.

Search normalization must preserve source wording and must not create multiple cultivar entities.

---

# 4. Botanical description

RHS, NC State, JC Raulston and archived Chicago converge on a compact slow dwarf with dense multibranching and tightly overlapping palmate leaves that create layered or tufted branch canopies. [SRC-RC-010-001–004]

**G4 synthesis:** the diagnostic effect depends on architecture, scale and leaf arrangement together, not an isolated leaf.

Quantitative claims remain source-bounded where accession and measurement protocols are absent.

---

# 5. Whole-plant architecture

RHS describes spreading branches and dwarf grouping; NC State dense multistemmed form; Chicago upright form; JC Raulston tree form and a measured six-foot accession. These are compatible dimensions of a compact plant and demonstrate that “dwarf” is not a fixed height ceiling. [SRC-RC-010-001–004]

Pruning, age, grafting and viewing angle can alter visible layering. Architecture remains separate from individual leaf morphology.

---

# 6. Leaf morphology

RHS reports five or seven lobes; NC State and Chicago provide broader five-to-nine-lobe fields. The record preserves the source difference and emphasizes tight overlap rather than a universal lobe count. [SRC-RC-010-001–004]

Layering must be documented at branch and whole-plant scale; an isolated leaf is insufficient. Standardized authenticated multi-accession measurements remain unavailable.

---

# 7. Seasonal characteristics

Spring foliage is pale or yellow-green, summer bright-to-medium green and autumn gold, orange, scarlet or red. Exact range varies. RHS reports green flowers while archived Chicago reports reddish-purple; the conflict is retained. [SRC-RC-010-001–004, SRC-RC-010-006]

Red-flushed samaras are secondary characters. Seasonal colours are trajectories rather than fixed values.

---

# 8. Horticultural information

Moist freely draining soil and full sun to partial shade are supported, with JC Raulston light-shade context. NC State’s relative heat-tolerance wording is qualified by temperature, drought, wind and scorch cautions. [SRC-RC-010-001–004]

RHS H5, NC State 5b–9b and archived Chicago 6–9 remain separate frameworks. Species-level guidance is not silently promoted to cultivar-specific tolerance.

---

# 9. Historical provenance

JC Raulston documents accession 030154 received in 2003, planted in 2004 and measured at six feet in 2022. Maple Society North America records 2015 Maple of the Year recognition. These establish modern horticultural documentation, not origin. [SRC-RC-010-003, SRC-RC-010-005]

Original selector, date and introduction remain unresolved; commercial regional/discovery narratives are excluded without primary evidence.

---

# 10. Propagation and cultivar continuity

RHS publishes autumn layering, late-winter grafting or softwood cuttings. Vegetative propagation is required for continuity.

Seedlings do not inherit the named identity despite source-reported self-seeding. Propagation does not authenticate incorrectly identified source material.

---

# 11. Diagnostic framework

Higher-confidence description combines true dwarf/compact scale, dense multibranching and tightly overlapping leaves forming layered branch canopies.

RC-008 is the essential close comparator: ‘Shishi-gashira’ emphasizes crinkled leaves and close tufting on a taller dense plant, while RC-010 emphasizes dwarf scale and overlapping planar layers.

**Descriptive confidence:** high.  
**Clonal authentication from appearance alone:** low.
