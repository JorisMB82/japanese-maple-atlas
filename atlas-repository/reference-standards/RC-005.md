# 1. Executive Summary

*Acer shirasawanum* ‘Aureum’ is a slow-growing Japanese maple cultivated for its rounded, shallowly lobed foliage, which emerges bright yellow or golden yellow and generally remains yellow to yellow-green through the growing season. Its leaves are fuller and more nearly circular than the deeply divided or strongly pointed foliage commonly associated with many *Acer palmatum* cultivars. Crimson to reddish-purple flowers and red samaras are carried in upright clusters, providing an additional morphological and taxonomic distinction. is currently and appropriately placed under *Acer shirasawanum*. Kew’s Plants of the World Online accepts *Acer shirasawanum* Koidz. as a species, first published in 1911, with a native range in central and western Honshu and Shikoku. Historical horticultural sources, however, frequently treated ‘Aureum’ under *Acer japonicum*, and RHS continues to list *Acer japonicum* ‘Aureum’ and *A. japonicum* var. *aureum* as synonyms. annot be represented accurately by a single permanent color value. RHS describes bright yellow leaves throughout summer, Missouri Botanical Garden describes spring-yellow foliage darkening to yellow-green, and Oregon State describes bright yellow to greenish yellow depending on exposure. Autumn foliage may become golden yellow, orange, orange-red or reddish, but Oregon State reports that some plants show little autumn coloration. The governed trait is therefore a **condition-dependent seasonal color trajectory**, not simply “gold foliage.” has greater exposure sensitivity than a generic “full sun to partial shade” tag communicates. Oregon State recommends shade and warns of scorch in hot sun, late frost and wind. NC State similarly recommends afternoon shade to reduce scorch. The cultivar should consequently be modeled as a cool-site, protected-exposure plant whose tolerances depend materially on regional climate and water availability. # 13. Soil and Moisture

RHS recommends moist but well-drained acidic or neutral soils. NC State similarly recommends average moist, well-drained soil. ractical guidance

* avoid prolonged saturation;
* protect the shallow root zone with mulch;

* favor acidic to neutral conditions;

# 17. Propagation and Cultivar Continuity

Named-cultivar identity requires vegetative continuity.

A plant grown from seed produced by ‘Aureum’ is not automatically ‘Aureum’, even if it has yellow leaves.

## Approved identity rule

The cultivar name may be retained only when the plant is vegetatively derived from material accepted as belonging to the cultivar.

## Likely propagation method

Grafting is the customary commercial method for Japanese-maple cultivars. A plant-patent background discussing *Acer shirasawanum* ‘Aureum’ describes it as slow growing, difficult to propagate and difficult to graft successfully, although this is not equivalent to a controlled propagation study. ormulation

> ‘Aureum’ is normally maintained vegetatively, commonly through grafting, but propagation is regarded as comparatively difficult.

## Qualification

The audit did not recover:

* authenticated graft-success percentages;
* controlled rootstock comparisons;
* cutting-success data;
* or tissue-culture validation.

# 19. Diagnostic Framework

## 19.1 High-confidence descriptive combination

A strong descriptive identification includes:

1. slow-growing bushy shrub or small tree;
2. upright-rounded to broadly rounded crown;
3. broad, rounded or suborbicular leaves;
4. numerous shallow lobes;
5. bright yellow spring foliage;
6. yellow to yellow-green summer foliage;
7. upright crimson flower clusters;
8. conspicuous red samaras;
9. sensitivity to hot sun and drying wind.

## 19.2 Diagnostic limits

No single feature independently authenticates the cultivar.

The following are insufficient by themselves:

* yellow foliage;
* rounded leaves;
* red samaras;
* an old *Acer japonicum* label;
* a nursery tag;
* or resemblance to a photograph.

Other *Acer shirasawanum* cultivars and yellow-leaved maples may overlap in appearance.

# Part II — Public Atlas Profile

## *Acer shirasawanum* ‘Aureum’

### Golden full-moon maple

‘Aureum’ is a slow-growing Japanese maple with broad, rounded leaves that emerge brilliant yellow and generally remain yellow to yellow-green through summer. Unlike the more sharply divided leaves of many *Acer palmatum* cultivars, its foliage is full and nearly circular, with numerous shallow lobes. res

* **Habit:** Bushy, upright-rounded shrub or small tree
* **Height:** Potentially about 15–20 ft after many years
* **Spread:** Potentially similar to its height
* **Spring foliage:** Bright yellow to golden yellow, sometimes edged red
* **Summer foliage:** Yellow to yellow-green
* **Autumn foliage:** Gold, orange or orange-red, but variable
* **Leaves:** Rounded, shallowly lobed and finely serrated
* **Flowers:** Crimson to reddish purple in upright clusters
* **Fruit:** Red paired samaras that mature brown
* **Exposure:** Partial shade preferred
* **Soil:** Moist, rich and well-drained
* **Hardiness:** Generally suited to cool-temperate climates around USDA zones 5 or 6 through 7
* **Growth rate:** Slow

### Why it is distinctive

The cultivar combines:

* unusually luminous yellow foliage;
* rounded full-moon-type leaves;
* upright crimson flowers and red fruit;
* and a compact, slowly developing crown.

### Garden use

It is particularly effective:

* as an understory specimen;
* in a protected courtyard;
* beside darker evergreens;
* near a shaded terrace;
* in a woodland-edge planting;
* or where the foliage can be backlit without exposure to severe afternoon heat.

### Care note

Protect the plant from intense afternoon sun, drying wind and late spring frost. Keep the soil evenly moist but well drained. Hot, exposed conditions can scorch the pale foliage and may prevent a strong autumn display. note

The cultivar was historically known as *Acer japonicum* ‘Aureum’ or *A. japonicum* var. *aureum*. Modern taxonomic and horticultural authorities place it under *Acer shirasawanum*. ation caution

Yellow foliage alone does not prove that a tree is ‘Aureum’. Reliable identification should consider:

* rounded shallowly lobed leaves;
* seasonal color progression;
* upright reproductive clusters;
* growth habit;
* and documented vegetative provenance.
