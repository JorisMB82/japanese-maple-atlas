# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

## Published Description

The accepted working name is *Acer palmatum* ‘Seiryu’. The RHS presents the epithet as ‘Seiryū’ and recognizes ‘Seiryû’ as a spelling variant. NC State uses ‘Seiryu’ and records *Acer palmatum* var. *dissectum* ‘Seiryu’ as a previous formulation. Missouri Botanical Garden currently displays the plant under the varietal formulation *Acer palmatum* var. *dissectum* ‘Seiryu’.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS001–AS005

## Editorial Synthesis

The reviewed spellings refer to the same cultivated entity. The Atlas uses ‘Seiryu’ as its practical working form while preserving ‘Seiryū’, ‘Seiryû’ and historical *var. dissectum* formulations as source-specific nomenclatural records.

Historical use of *var. dissectum* does not establish that it is the preferred current botanical treatment. It must remain distinct from the modern horticultural concept of a dissectum classification group.

The original Japanese written form, naming author, intended transliteration and historical naming rationale remain unresolved.

---

# 3. Horticultural classification

## Published Description

The RHS places the cultivar within a horticultural dissectum category whose members have finely divided, feathery leaves with finely serrated margins. NC State uses the common name “lace-leaf Japanese maple” and describes the leaf shape as pinnatisect. Missouri Botanical Garden describes dissected forms as laceleaf, cutleaf or threadleaf Japanese maples.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS008–AS013

## Editorial Synthesis

The following terms are related but not interchangeable:

* **dissected:** a general leaf-morphology descriptor;
* **pinnatisect:** a more technical morphological descriptor used by NC State;
* **laceleaf:** a common horticultural term;
* **Dissectum Group:** a horticultural classification;
* **var. dissectum:** a historical or taxonomic formulation found in some records.

The cultivar’s leaf classification must remain separate from its whole-tree habit.

# 4. Diagnostic summary

## Editorial Synthesis

‘Seiryu’ is characterized by an unusual combination of:

* deeply dissected foliage;
* green rather than persistent purple-red summer coloration;
* upright whole-tree architecture;
* light-green spring growth commonly edged or tipped with red;
* and multicolored autumn foliage.

This combination is highly characteristic among commonly cultivated dissected Japanese maples.

It is not, by itself, proof of authenticated clonal identity.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS008, AS014, AS031–AS037, AS059–AS063

# 5. Whole-tree architecture

## Published Description

RHS describes ‘Seiryu’ as a medium-sized deciduous shrub of broadly columnar growth and classifies its habit as columnar upright. NC State records an erect, vase-shaped form. Missouri Botanical Garden contrasts ‘Seiryu’ with the generally low, rounded or cascading form of many dissected maples.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS014–AS019

## Editorial Synthesis

The most defensible normalized description is:

> An upright Japanese maple with an erect, vase-shaped to broadly columnar crown.

The architectural terms are compatible but not identical:

* **upright** describes the general vertical growth tendency;
* **erect** describes branch or shoot orientation;
* **vase-shaped** describes a crown widening upward;
* **broadly columnar** describes an elongated but not narrowly fastigiate crown outline.

The available evidence does not establish that every plant maintains precisely the same outline at every age.

## Expert Interpretation

Rootstock, graft position, formative pruning, exposure, crowding and individual development may alter the apparent crown form. These factors should be considered when assessing unidentified material.

# 6. Morphology and architecture as separate dimensions

## Validation Finding

‘Seiryu’ confirms that dissected foliage and upright growth are not contradictory.

“Dissected” describes the structure of the leaves.

“Upright,” “vase-shaped,” “columnar,” “cascading” and “weeping” describe the architecture of the plant.

A dissected cultivar may therefore be upright, while another dissected cultivar may be cascading or pendulous.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS018–AS019

**Validation record:** VL-RC002-001

**Architecture impact:** None.

This was the principal representational test assigned to RC-002, and the existing ontology handled it successfully.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS020–AS026

## Editorial Synthesis

A practical North American landscape expectation is approximately:

* **height:** 10–15 feet;
* **spread:** 8–12 feet.

The RHS estimate of 2.5–4 metres broadly overlaps with these North American estimates.

These figures are published horticultural estimates, not fixed biological limits. They should not be interpreted as proving that no authenticated plant can become smaller or larger.

## Expert Interpretation

* climate;
* growing season;
* soil;
* irrigation;
* rootstock;
* graft height;
* exposure;
* pruning;
* competition;
* and plant age.

The evidence is insufficient to approve a fixed annual growth increment.

# 8. Leaf morphology

## Published Description

RHS describes light-green leaves with deeply incised lobes and identifies the dissectum group as having fine, feathery, deeply divided leaves. NC State classifies the leaves as simple, pinnatisect, lobed and approximately one to three inches in length and width. Missouri Botanical Garden describes deeply cut leaves to approximately two inches long and states that they are smaller than those of most other dissectum cultivars.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS027–AS030

## Editorial Synthesis

The leaves may be described as:

* deeply dissected;
* deeply incised;
* fine-textured;
* feathery;
* or lace-like.

The word “fine” describes the visual effect created by narrow, divided leaf segments. It is not a quantified anatomical measurement.

Published leaf-size figures are broadly compatible, but they lack:

* identified accession;
* sample size;
* developmental stage;
* measurement protocol;
* and environmental context.

Leaf size should therefore remain a source-reported range rather than a definitive cultivar measurement.

# 13. Cultivation

## Cultivar-specific Published Recommendations

RHS recommends moist but well-drained soil, acidic or neutral pH, full sun or partial shade, and a sheltered position. It states that leaf color is best in partial shade, while full sun may be tolerated, and warns that insufficient moisture or excessive exposure can cause scorch.

NC State recommends well-drained, acidic, humus-rich soils in full sun to partial shade and advises afternoon shade in hot-summer regions to reduce scorch. It lists USDA zones 5a–8b.

Missouri Botanical Garden recommends moist, organically rich, slightly acidic, well-drained soil. It advises afternoon shade in the southern portion of the growing range, consistent moisture, mulch and protection from strong winds.

**Assertion references:** RC002-AS047–AS054

## Species- and Group-derived Guidance

RHS identifies frost, drought, waterlogging, drying winds and hot sun as common causes of Japanese-maple leaf scorch. It also states that cultivars with heavily dissected foliage are particularly prone.

This guidance is inherited from the species or morphological group and must not be misrepresented as a cultivar-specific comparative trial.

## Editorial Synthesis

The most defensible practical guidance is:

* plant in moist but freely draining soil;
* avoid both prolonged waterlogging and repeated drought;
* provide protection from drying wind;
* consider afternoon shade in hot climates;
* maintain reliable moisture during heat;
* and avoid assuming that partial shade eliminates scorch risk.

Full sun may be suitable in cooler regions or favorable sites, but exposure recommendations are climate-dependent.

# 15. Comparative interpretation

## Published Comparative Description

NC State describes ‘Seiryu’ as the only dissected form of Japanese maple with an upright habit. Missouri Botanical Garden similarly describes it as the only dissected cultivar with an upright form.

**Assertion reference:** RC002-AS059

## Editorial Synthesis

The Atlas does not adopt an unrestricted universal statement.

The approved formulation is:

> ‘Seiryu’ is an exceptional upright-growing cultivar among the commonly recognized dissected Japanese maples.

This reflects the strong institutional consensus without claiming that an exhaustive, timeless global review of every cultivar has been completed.

## Diagnostic Interpretation

Upright architecture is highly characteristic in the context of commonly cultivated laceleaf maples.

However:

* partly upright cultivars may exist;
* pruning and training may alter apparent habit;
* seedlings may resemble the cultivar;
* and commercial material may be mislabeled.

The habit is diagnostically useful, not independently authenticating.

# Part VI — Concise Public Atlas Profile

## *Acer palmatum* ‘Seiryu’

### Upright laceleaf Japanese maple

‘Seiryu’ is an unusual green-leaved Japanese maple that combines finely dissected, lace-like foliage with an upright tree form. Most familiar laceleaf maples develop low, spreading or cascading crowns, while ‘Seiryu’ grows erectly, commonly forming a vase-shaped to broadly columnar plant.

### Key features

* **Habit:** Upright, erect and vase-shaped to broadly columnar
* **Height:** Commonly about 10–15 ft
* **Spread:** Commonly about 8–12 ft
* **Spring foliage:** Light green with reddish tips or margins
* **Summer foliage:** Green to medium green
* **Autumn foliage:** Gold, yellow, orange and red
* **Leaves:** Deeply dissected, fine and lace-like
* **Flowers:** Small, reddish, in spring
* **Fruit:** Winged samaras
* **Hardiness:** Generally listed for USDA zones 5–8
* **Light:** Full sun to partial shade, with afternoon shade often beneficial in hot climates
* **Soil:** Moist but well-drained

### Why it is distinctive

Its defining landscape feature is the combination of laceleaf foliage and upright architecture. This makes it useful where the delicate texture of a dissected maple is desired without the low, cascading form common to many laceleaf cultivars.

### Garden use

It can work as:

* a small specimen tree;
* an architectural accent;
* a courtyard tree;
* a plant for a Japanese-inspired garden;
* or a focal plant in a relatively compact space.

### Care note

Protect it from severe drying winds and avoid allowing the soil to become either persistently waterlogged or severely dry. In hot-summer climates, afternoon shade and consistent moisture may reduce leaf scorch. Heavily dissected Japanese maples are generally considered especially susceptible to scorch under stressful conditions.

### Identification caution

The combination of upright growth and deeply dissected green foliage is highly characteristic, but appearance or a nursery label alone cannot confirm authenticated clonal identity.

### Historical note

An RHS herbarium specimen recorded as ‘Seiryu’ was collected in Surrey, United Kingdom, in 1989. The cultivar’s original selector, naming history and earliest introduction remain unresolved.
