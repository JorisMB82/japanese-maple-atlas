# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

RHS maintains accepted profiles linking ‘Butterfly’ and ‘Kochō-no-mai’; Missouri Botanical Garden and UConn use ‘Butterfly’; Kew supports species placement only. The Atlas retains ‘Butterfly’ as the stable working name and the RHS relationship as institutional synonym evidence. [SRC-RC-007-001–004]

The historical sequence, original written form and provenance of ‘Kochō-no-mai’ remain unresolved. RHS maintains ‘White Butterfly’ separately, so it must not be collapsed into this record. [SRC-RC-007-001, SRC-RC-007-007]

The following states remain separate: traceable vegetatively propagated material; material carrying a label without independent authentication; seed-derived offspring; green shoots within a variegated plant; and other marginally variegated cultivars.

**Authentication limit:** this Reference Standard defines a conservative descriptive and evidentiary profile. It does not certify an individual specimen, a nursery label or commercial clonal uniformity.

---

# 3. Naming variants and synonyms

The accepted working name is *Acer palmatum* ‘Butterfly’. ‘Kochō-no-mai’ is retained as an RHS-recorded synonym and institutional display form. ‘Kocho-no-mai’ and spacing variants are search forms, not separate cultivar entities. ‘White Butterfly’ remains explicitly separate. ‘Kocho Nishiki’ is not approved as a synonym.

Search normalization may improve retrieval, but it must not erase source wording or convert unresolved history into proven equivalence.

Owner acceptance is required for use of the RHS-recorded ‘Kochō-no-mai’ relationship while its historical sequence and primary provenance remain unresolved.

# 4. Botanical description

RHS and UConn support five- to seven-lobed foliage with green or grey-green centres, irregular cream-white margins and frequent pink tinting. Missouri Botanical Garden supports small-tree form and spring flowers. [SRC-RC-007-001–003]

**G4 synthesis:** ‘Butterfly’ is a rather upright variegated Japanese maple defined by irregular pale marginal tissue rather than a fixed pattern on every leaf.

Quantitative morphology remains limited where sources do not identify accession, developmental stage, sample size, leaf position, measurement protocol, rootstock or environmental context.

---

# 5. Whole-plant architecture

RHS describes a rather upright bushy shrub or small tree; published dimensions differ but generally support a slender plant narrower than tall. [SRC-RC-007-001–003]

Stem number, pruning, graft height, exposure and age may alter form. It is not governed as cascading, columnar or intrinsically dwarf.

Architecture and individual leaf morphology remain separate analytical dimensions.

---

# 6. Leaf morphology

The controlled core is five- to seven-lobed palmate foliage with green to grey-green centres and irregular cream-white margins, often pink-tinged. Pattern width and pink intensity are variable. [SRC-RC-007-001, SRC-RC-007-003]

The institutional sources do not quantify reversion frequency. Green shoots are therefore horticultural observations requiring context, not automatic new cultivars or a proven constant diagnostic event.

Descriptive confidence is higher for the combined profile than for authentication from any individual leaf character.

---

# 7. Seasonal characteristics

Spring and summer marginal variegation are well supported. The RHS seasonal presentation and Missouri Botanical Garden’s “Good Fall” flag do not establish one exact autumn sequence, so stronger autumn-colour claims remain unresolved. [SRC-RC-007-001–003]

Photography, exposure, leaf age and processing can materially alter apparent cream, green and pink contrast.

Seasonal descriptions are trajectories and source-bounded observations, not permanent colour values or performance guarantees.

---

# 8. Horticultural information

Partial shade, moist freely draining soil and shelter from excessive heat or wind are supported. Full sun is contextual; moisture deficit and hot exposure increase scorch risk. [SRC-RC-007-001–003]

RHS H6 and Missouri Botanical Garden USDA 5–8 remain separate frameworks.

Species-level guidance is not silently promoted to cultivar-specific tolerance.

---

# 9. Historical provenance

RHS herbarium specimens dated April, June and October 2000 and JC Raulston photograph records document modern institutional use. They do not establish original selection or clonal uniformity. [SRC-RC-007-005–006]

Claims of 1882/1938 introduction or ‘Kocho Nishiki’ equivalence remain excluded without primary locators.

Modern institutional records document use or asserted identity, not original-clone status.

---

# 10. Propagation and cultivar continuity

RHS publishes grafting or softwood cuttings; vegetative propagation is required for continuity.

Seedlings do not inherit the named cultivar identity. Propagation preserves a selected genotype only when source material is correctly identified; the method itself is not authentication.

---

# 11. Diagnostic framework

Useful description combines irregular cream-white marginal variegation, frequent pink tinting and upright/bushy architecture. One variegated leaf, one label or one photograph is insufficient for authentication.

Comparisons are RC-005 for yellow foliage versus variegation, RC-009 for seasonal marginal colour versus structural variegation, and RC-003 for upright form with a different ornamental domain.

**Descriptive confidence:** high for the governed combination where stated.  
**Clonal authentication from appearance alone:** low.
