# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

RHS displays ‘Ōsakazuki’ and marks the cultivar name accepted; Missouri Botanical Garden and NC State use ‘Osakazuki’. Kew supports *Acer palmatum* at species rank only. The Atlas retains ‘Osakazuki’ as the stable working form and ‘Ōsakazuki’ as a traceable institutional romanization. [SRC-RC-006-001–004]

‘Taihai’ remains an RHS-recorded synonym whose historical scope is not independently established. Amoenum, Elegans and Heptalobum are preserved as source-specific horticultural-group formulations, not as separate cultivar identities or clonal proof. JC Raulston’s ‘Ōsakasuki’ is treated as an unresolved record spelling, not an approved synonym. [SRC-RC-006-001–002, SRC-RC-006-006]

The following states remain separate: traceable vegetatively propagated material; material carrying a label without independent authentication; seed-derived offspring; and morphologically similar cultivars or unidentified plants.

**Authentication limit:** this Reference Standard defines a conservative descriptive and evidentiary profile. It does not certify an individual specimen, a nursery label or commercial clonal uniformity.

---

# 3. Naming variants and synonyms

The accepted working name is *Acer palmatum* ‘Osakazuki’. Source-recorded synonyms, romanizations, spacing forms and horticultural-group terms are retained only at the confidence and scope stated above.

Search normalization may improve retrieval, but it must not create new cultivar entities, erase source wording or convert unresolved historical relationships into proven equivalence.

Owner acceptance is required for the conservative treatment of ‘Taihai’, the unresolved group formulations, the ‘Ōsakasuki’ discrepancy and the missing primary provenance.

# 4. Botanical description

Institutional descriptions converge on a green-leaved large shrub or small tree with relatively large palmate leaves, usually seven serrate lobes, and marked autumn colour. Small spring flowers and red samaras are secondary characters. [SRC-RC-006-001–003]

**G4 synthesis:** ‘Osakazuki’ is a rounded-to-upright green Japanese maple whose broad palmate foliage remains green through the growing season before turning orange-scarlet, crimson or red. This is a descriptive profile, not an authentication test.

Quantitative morphology remains limited where reviewed sources do not identify accession, developmental stage, sample size, leaf position, measurement protocol, rootstock or environmental context.

---

# 5. Whole-plant architecture

RHS describes a large rounded/bushy shrub; Missouri Botanical Garden a vigorous upright tree with rounded crown; NC State a rounded shrub or tree. These terms are compatible when growth direction, crown outline and horticultural form are kept distinct. [SRC-RC-006-001–003]

Published dimensions remain contextual rather than a universal maximum. JC Raulston accession measurements are observations of labelled living material, not a growth-rate rule. [SRC-RC-006-006]

Architecture and individual leaf morphology remain separate analytical dimensions. Crown form may be altered by age, stem number, pruning, competition, exposure, rootstock and graft height.

---

# 6. Leaf morphology

The controlled core is a simple palmate green leaf, usually seven-lobed, serrate and moderately divided. Missouri Botanical Garden’s reported basal cupping is retained at moderate confidence because multi-accession frequency is unknown. [SRC-RC-006-001–003]

Seven lobes, large size or cupping alone cannot identify the cultivar.

Descriptive confidence is higher for the combined profile than for authentication from any individual leaf character.

---

# 7. Seasonal characteristics

Spring and summer foliage are green. Autumn descriptions converge on brilliant orange-scarlet, red or crimson, while exact colour and duration depend on site, weather and observation date. [SRC-RC-006-001–003]

Flowers and samaras are documented but weak diagnostically. No permanent digital colour value or guaranteed annual performance is approved.

Seasonal descriptions are trajectories and source-bounded observations, not permanent colour values or performance guarantees.

---

# 8. Horticultural information

Moist, freely draining soil and climate-qualified sun to partial shade are supported. RHS and Missouri Botanical Garden warn about scorch under moisture deficit or excessive exposure; NC State’s stronger resistance wording is therefore not converted into an absolute cultivar claim. [SRC-RC-006-001–003]

RHS H6, Missouri Botanical Garden USDA 5–9 and NC State 5a–8b remain separate frameworks.

Cultivar-specific statements are preferred. Species-level guidance is not silently promoted to cultivar-specific tolerance, and hardiness frameworks are not merged into a guarantee.

---

# 9. Historical provenance

RHS herbarium records document material under the cultivar name from 1996–2004, and JC Raulston records modern living accessions. These establish institutional use, not original-clone provenance. [SRC-RC-006-005–006]

Original selection, date, naming authority, Japanese characters, earliest catalogue and the historical ‘Taihai’ relationship remain unresolved.

Modern herbarium, living-collection and photograph records document institutional use or asserted identity. They do not, without determination history and provenance, establish original-clone status.

---

# 10. Propagation and cultivar continuity

RHS publishes grafting or softwood cuttings; vegetative propagation supports continuity but does not authenticate a plant.

Seedlings do not inherit the named cultivar identity. Grafting, cuttings or layering preserve a selected genotype only when the source material is correctly identified; the propagation method itself is not authentication.

---

# 11. Diagnostic framework

Higher-confidence description uses the combination of broad green usually seven-lobed foliage, rounded-to-upright crown and intense autumn colour. Authentication requires provenance or determination evidence beyond appearance.

Useful comparisons are RC-001 for summer-foliage contrast, RC-002/RC-004 for leaf dissection, RC-003 for bark-led versus foliage-led value, RC-005 for species and growing-season colour, and RC-009 for spring-led versus autumn-led seasonality.

**Descriptive confidence:** high for the governed combination where stated.  
**Clonal authentication from appearance alone:** low.

# 17. Rejected claims

2. Seven lobes or a cupped base alone prove identity.
3. Every ‘Taihai’ and ‘Osakazuki’ label is proven clonally equivalent.
4. Horticultural-group formulations are automatically interchangeable.
5. The cultivar has one fixed mature size or universal growth rate.
6. Full sun cannot cause scorch.
7. One institutional or nursery label conclusively authenticates a plant.
8. ‘Ōsakasuki’ is an approved synonym.
9. Seedlings inherit the cultivar identity.
