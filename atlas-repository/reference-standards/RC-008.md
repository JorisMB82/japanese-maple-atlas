# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

## Published Description

The Royal Horticultural Society accepts *Acer palmatum* ‘Shishi-gashira’ and records ‘Ribesifolium’, ‘Crispifolium’ and ‘Mejishi’ as synonyms. Missouri Botanical Garden and JC Raulston Arboretum use the unhyphenated form ‘Shishigashira’. [RC008-AS001–AS004; SRC-RC-008-001–003]

Kew accepts *Acer palmatum* Thunb. at species rank. Species acceptance supports botanical placement but does not establish cultivar origin or clonal history. [RC008-AS005; SRC-RC-008-004]

## Editorial Synthesis

The Atlas accepts:

> *Acer palmatum* ‘Shishi-gashira’

as the stable working form approved at G0. The unhyphenated ‘Shishigashira’ is retained as a search and institutional spelling variant. The RHS-recorded synonym set is preserved without assuming that all names have identical historical scope or were introduced simultaneously.

Four states must remain distinct:

1. vegetatively propagated material with traceable accession or nursery provenance;
2. material labelled ‘Shishi-gashira’ or a recorded synonym without independent authentication;
3. seed-derived offspring;
4. other compact maples with crowded, curled or tufted foliage.

## Authentication limit

This record defines a conservative descriptive profile. A cultivar label, “lion’s mane” common name, photograph or single leaf does not independently authenticate an individual specimen.

# 3. Naming variants and synonyms

## Source-recorded names

RHS records:

- *Acer palmatum* ‘Ribesifolium’;
- *Acer palmatum* ‘Crispifolium’;
- *Acer palmatum* ‘Mejishi’.

RHS also maintains a related page headed ‘Ribesifolium’ while identifying the common horticultural plant as Japanese maple ‘Shishi-gashira’. Missouri Botanical Garden states that ‘Shishigashira’ is synonymous with ‘Ribesifolium’. [RC008-AS002–AS004; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

## Governed treatment

- **‘Shishi-gashira’** — accepted Atlas working form.
- **‘Shishigashira’** — unhyphenated institutional and search variant.
- **‘Ribesifolium’** — RHS- and Missouri-recorded synonym; historical basis and priority unresolved.
- **‘Crispifolium’** — RHS-recorded synonym; etymological relationship to crinkled foliage is plausible but not primary historical evidence.
- **‘Mejishi’** — RHS-recorded synonym; exact historical and linguistic relationship unresolved.
- **“Shish geshira”** — typographical error in current RHS descriptive prose; explicitly rejected and not propagated.

## Name interpretation

Missouri Botanical Garden reports that “Shishigashira” means lion’s head and notes the common name lion’s-head maple. This is retained as a source-reported interpretation. The Atlas has not recovered an original naming statement or primary Japanese historical source. [RC008-AS006; SRC-RC-008-002]

## Editorial rule

Search normalization may support hyphenated and unhyphenated forms, but the synonym field must preserve institutional names rather than collapse them into one undifferentiated spelling string.

# 4. Botanical description

## Published Description

RHS describes a dense bushy shrub of distinctive upright habit with masses of small dark-green lobed leaves. Missouri Botanical Garden describes an upright, compact, slow-growing form with bright-green, five- to seven-lobed, crinkled leaves to approximately 2 inches long, growing in tufts along the stems. [RC008-AS007–AS011; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

Missouri Botanical Garden also notes that foliage on young trees may appear sparse. Small reddish-purple spring flowers are followed by samaras. [RC008-AS012–AS013; SRC-RC-008-002]

## Editorial Synthesis

> ‘Shishi-gashira’ is a slow-growing, dense upright Japanese maple with a compact to bushy crown. Its small five- to seven-lobed green leaves are distinctly crinkled or curled and are held closely in tufts along stems, creating the characteristic crowded “lion’s mane” texture.

The visible texture is a combined architectural and morphological effect. It must not be represented as leaf shape alone.

## Scope limits

The present evidence does not provide a standardized authenticated multi-accession description of:

- internode length;
- number of leaves per apparent tuft;
- leaf-blade measurements beyond the Missouri estimate;
- degree and direction of curling;
- petiole length and colour;
- bud-break date across climates;
- crown density by age;
- rootstock or graft-height effects;
- or samara measurements.

# 5. Whole-plant architecture

## Published Description

RHS describes a dense bushy shrub with distinctive upright habit. Missouri Botanical Garden describes an upright, compact tree. JC Raulston lists tree form, a 15-foot general size and an accession measured at 8 ft on 9 November 2023. [RC008-AS007, RC008-AS014–AS017; SRC-RC-008-001–003]

## Editorial Synthesis

The normalized architectural description is:

> A dense, bushy and upright shrub or small tree with compact crown development and closely presented foliage.

The terms are complementary:

- **upright** — principal direction of growth;
- **compact** — reduced extension and close branching relative to many larger upright cultivars;
- **bushy/dense** — crown texture and branching presentation;
- **shrub or small tree** — horticultural expression rather than different identities.

The cultivar is not naturally pendulous, cascading or broadly spreading in the manner of a dissectum. Young plants may appear less densely foliated than mature examples.

## Published dimensions

- RHS: 2.5–4 m high, 1.5–2.5 m spread and 20–50 years to stated maturity;
- Missouri Botanical Garden: 7–15 ft high and 5–12 ft wide over a number of years;
- JC Raulston: summary size 15 ft, with accession xx0090 measured at 8 ft on 9 November 2023. [RC008-AS014–AS017; SRC-RC-008-001–003]

These are contextual estimates and records. No universal maximum, crown ratio or annual growth increment is approved.

# 6. Leaf morphology

## Published Description

RHS describes masses of small dark-green lobed leaves. Missouri Botanical Garden describes bright-green palmate leaves with five to seven lobes, a crinkled texture and a length up to approximately 2 inches, held in tufts along stems. [RC008-AS008–AS011; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

## Editorial Synthesis

The controlled leaf profile is:

- simple, palmate leaf;
- five to seven lobes in the Missouri cultivar description;
- small blade relative to many ordinary upright *Acer palmatum* cultivars;
- bright to dark green growing-season colour;
- crinkled, curled or compacted surface/edge presentation;
- close, tufted arrangement along stems.

“Tufted” describes the visual grouping of leaves along shortened or close stem intervals. It must not be interpreted as botanical whorling without direct node-level evidence.

## Comparison limit

The small crinkled leaves distinguish the cultivar descriptively from RC-002 ‘Seiryu’, whose fine texture results from deep dissection, and from proposed RC-010 ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’, whose product role is layered, overlapping foliage on a dwarf framework. Authenticated comparative measurements are still needed.

# 7. Seasonal characteristics

## Spring

Missouri Botanical Garden states that the cultivar tends to leaf out early and may be damaged by late spring frost. Small reddish-purple flowers appear in spring. [RC008-AS012–AS013, RC008-AS018; SRC-RC-008-002]

**Governed synthesis:**

> Spring foliage is bright to dark green and may emerge early. Early emergence is a horticultural risk factor, not an authentication character by itself.

## Summer

RHS and Missouri Botanical Garden support dense green foliage, with small crinkled leaves held in masses or tufts. [RC008-AS008–AS011; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

**Governed synthesis:**

> Summer foliage is green and visually dense, with the characteristic crowded, curled texture strongest when branch architecture and leaf arrangement are visible together.

## Autumn

RHS describes soft orange autumn foliage. Missouri Botanical Garden reports showy shades of gold and crimson. [RC008-AS019; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

**Governed synthesis:**

> Autumn foliage ranges from gold and soft orange to crimson. Exact colour, sequence, intensity and duration depend on climate, exposure, plant condition and observation date.

One exact autumn colour is not approved.

## Flowers and fruit

Small reddish-purple flowers are followed by samaras that ripen from late summer into autumn in the Missouri account. Both overlap broadly with other *Acer palmatum* material and are weak diagnostic features. [RC008-AS013; SRC-RC-008-002]

## Winter interest

No distinctive cultivar-specific bark colour is established. Winter branch density may reveal the compact upright framework but is not independently diagnostic.

# 8. Horticultural information

## Light and exposure

RHS permits full sun or partial shade. Missouri Botanical Garden permits full sun to part shade, with afternoon shade recommended in hotter southern parts of the range. [RC008-AS020; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

**Editorial synthesis:**

> Use full sun to partial shade according to regional heat, wind and soil moisture. In hot climates, afternoon shade and reliable moisture are prudent.

## Soil and moisture

RHS recommends moist but well-drained acid or neutral soil. Missouri Botanical Garden recommends moist, organically rich, slightly acidic, well-drained soil and notes chlorosis risk in high-pH conditions. [RC008-AS021–AS022; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

**Editorial synthesis:**

> Provide moist but freely draining, organically enriched soil. Avoid chronic drought, waterlogging and strongly alkaline conditions.

## Scorch and frost

Missouri Botanical Garden warns that new foliage may scorch in hot full-sun conditions where soil moisture is inconsistent. It also reports early leaf-out and late-spring-frost risk. [RC008-AS018, RC008-AS023; SRC-RC-008-002]

The Atlas rejects absolute sun-tolerance and frost-immunity claims. Regional conditions materially affect performance.

## Hardiness

- RHS: H6;
- Missouri Botanical Garden: USDA zones 5–8. [RC008-AS024; SRC-RC-008-001–002]

These ratings use different frameworks and are not guarantees. Winter wind, late frost, drainage, container culture, root exposure, rootstock and establishment remain relevant.

## Growth and use

Missouri Botanical Garden describes slow growth and lists specimen, screen, woodland-edge, container and bonsai uses. Use categories are horticultural suggestions, not diagnostic evidence. [RC008-AS015, RC008-AS025; SRC-RC-008-002]

# 9. Historical provenance

## Documented institutional record

RHS herbarium records include:

- WSY0056505, collected 24 September 1982;
- WSY0032642, collected 14 April 2000;
- WSY0032641, collected 18 October 2000.

The labels use the unhyphenated ‘Shishigashira’ form while the current RHS title uses ‘Shishi-gashira’. [RC008-AS026; SRC-RC-008-005]

JC Raulston maintains a living-collection record under ‘Shishigashira’ and a photograph collection containing forty dated images from multiple seasons and locations. [RC008-AS017, RC008-AS027; SRC-RC-008-003, SRC-RC-008-006]

## Editorial Synthesis

These records demonstrate established modern institutional use by 1982 and substantial later observation. They do not establish:

- original selector;
- selection site;
- selection date;
- naming author;
- earliest Japanese catalogue;
- first international distribution;
- or the original historical relationship among ‘Shishi-gashira’, ‘Ribesifolium’, ‘Crispifolium’ and ‘Mejishi’.

## Name-history qualification

The “lion’s head” interpretation is retained as a source-reported meaning. It must not be expanded into an unsupported origin story.

# 10. Propagation and cultivar continuity

## Evidence position

The reviewed dedicated cultivar profiles do not provide a detailed cultivar-specific propagation protocol. As a named cultivar, continuity requires vegetative propagation; seed-derived offspring must not automatically inherit the cultivar identity.

## Editorial Synthesis

A continuity assessment should consider:

- documented scion or accession source;
- supplier and graft history;
- repeated seasonal expression;
- comparison with established material;
- and specialist review where synonym or phenotype claims conflict.

Propagation method alone does not authenticate a specimen. Rootstock, graft height, pruning and culture may modify crown and vigour without necessarily indicating a different cultivar.

## Unresolved continuity question

No comprehensive study of current commercial clonal uniformity or synonym-line equivalence was located.

# 11. Diagnostic framework

## Useful descriptive combination

A probable descriptive match may combine:

- dense, bushy and upright architecture;
- slow, compact development;
- small bright- to dark-green palmate leaves;
- five to seven lobes;
- crinkled or curled leaf texture;
- close tufting along stems;
- gold/orange to crimson autumn colour;
- and possible early spring emergence.

## Authentication limit

The following do not independently authenticate ‘Shishi-gashira’:

- one crinkled leaf;
- a dense crown;
- the “lion’s head” common name;
- orange autumn colour;
- an unhyphenated label;
- an RHS synonym;
- a nursery label;
- or one photograph. [RC008-AS028–AS030; SRC-RC-008-001–006]

## Confusion risks

- proposed RC-010 ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’, with layered and overlapping foliage on a more dwarf framework;
- other compact or dwarfed *Acer palmatum* cultivars;
- leaves distorted by stress, pests or herbicide injury;
- close crops that hide whole-plant scale and branch arrangement;
- synonym labels used without provenance.

## Comparative evidence needed

Higher-confidence separation would benefit from standardized measurement of:

- internode length;
- leaf length and width;
- lobe proportions;
- curl/crinkle angle;
- leaf number per stem interval;
- crown height/spread by age;
- bud-break timing;
- and same-scale comparisons with ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’ and other compact cultivars.

## Diagnostic conclusion

**Descriptive confidence:** high.  
**Authentication from morphology alone:** low to moderate.
