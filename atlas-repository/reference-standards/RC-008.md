# 2. Cultivar identity and nomenclature

RHS accepts the hyphenated ‘Shishi-gashira’ and records ‘Ribesifolium’, ‘Crispifolium’ and ‘Mejishi’ as synonyms. Missouri Botanical Garden and JC Raulston use ‘Shishigashira’. The Atlas retains the G0-locked hyphenated form and preserves the unhyphenated form for search. [SRC-RC-008-001–003]

The RHS prose form “Shish geshira” is an evident typographical error and is rejected. The three synonym relationships are directly recorded but their historical scope and priority remain unresolved. [SRC-RC-008-001–002]

Traceable vegetatively propagated material, labelled but unauthenticated material, seedlings, and other compact curled-leaf maples remain separate identity states.

**Authentication limit:** neither a common name, cultivar label, photograph nor one curled leaf authenticates an individual specimen.

---

# 3. Naming variants and synonyms

The accepted working name is *Acer palmatum* ‘Shishi-gashira’. ‘Shishigashira’ is a search/institutional variant. ‘Ribesifolium’, ‘Crispifolium’ and ‘Mejishi’ remain RHS-recorded synonyms with unresolved priority and historical scope. “Shish geshira” is not a valid variant.

Search normalization must not create separate cultivar entities or imply fully reconstructed synonym history.

Owner acceptance is required for retaining the three RHS-recorded synonyms while their priority, historical scope and primary provenance remain unresolved.

---

# 4. Botanical description

Institutional descriptions converge on a dense upright/compact plant with small five- to seven-lobed green leaves that are crinkled or curled and closely tufted along stems. [SRC-RC-008-001–003]

**G4 synthesis:** the characteristic “lion’s mane” effect is produced by leaf morphology, close leaf placement and whole-plant density together.

Quantitative claims remain source-bounded where accession and measurement protocols are absent.

---

# 5. Whole-plant architecture

RHS uses dense, bushy and upright; Missouri Botanical Garden uses upright, compact and slow-growing; JC Raulston records tree form. These terms describe different dimensions rather than different identities. [SRC-RC-008-001–003]

Young plants may appear sparser; age, pruning and branch density affect the visible texture. Architecture and individual leaf morphology remain separate dimensions.

---

# 6. Leaf morphology

Small five- to seven-lobed leaves are crinkled or curled and held closely in tufts. Missouri Botanical Garden’s approximate two-inch statement remains source-specific rather than a universal measurement. [SRC-RC-008-001–002]

Crinkling of one leaf is insufficient: diagnosis requires arrangement and architecture. Standardized authenticated measurements remain unavailable.

---

# 7. Seasonal characteristics

Green spring/summer foliage and autumn change are well supported. RHS reports soft orange; Missouri Botanical Garden reports gold and crimson. The governed range is gold/orange through crimson, qualified by conditions. [SRC-RC-008-001–002, SRC-RC-008-005–006]

Early leaf-out and frost exposure remain contextual horticultural considerations. Seasonal colours are trajectories, not fixed values.

---

# 8. Horticultural information

Moist, freely draining, organically rich soil and full sun to partial shade are supported; afternoon shade and reliable moisture are prudent in hot climates. High-pH chlorosis and typical Japanese-maple scorch risks remain contextual. [SRC-RC-008-001–003]

RHS H6 and USDA 5–8 remain separate source frameworks. Species-level guidance is not silently promoted to cultivar-specific tolerance.

---

# 9. Historical provenance

A 24 September 1982 RHS specimen and 2000 specimens document modern use under the unhyphenated label; JC Raulston living and photograph records document additional institutional material. None proves original-clone provenance. [SRC-RC-008-003, SRC-RC-008-005–006]

Original selection, introduction and synonym priority remain unresolved.

---

# 10. Propagation and cultivar continuity

Vegetative propagation is required for cultivar continuity; the reviewed institutional profiles do not establish one exclusive cultivar-specific method.

Seedlings do not inherit the named identity, and propagation does not authenticate incorrectly identified source material.

---

# 11. Diagnostic framework

Higher-confidence description combines dense upright/compact architecture, small crinkled leaves and close tufting along stems. This separates it from RC-010, where dwarf scale and overlapping layered leaves dominate.

Comparisons with RC-002 distinguish crowded small leaves from deep dissection; RC-003 compares upright form with bark-led value.

**Descriptive confidence:** high.  
**Clonal authentication from appearance alone:** low.
