## 3. Principal conflict and uncertainty register

### Spelling

The G0-approved Atlas form follows RHS: ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’. Spaced and title-case forms remain searchable source variants, not separate cultivars.

### Size and dwarf status

RHS and JCRA general summaries cluster near 3–4 ft/1–1.2 m, but NC State gives 4–6 ft and the documented JCRA accession reached 6 ft. “Dwarf” is therefore relative to the species and growth rate, not a fixed ceiling.

### Architecture

RHS says spreading branched; CBG says upright; NC State says dense and multistemmed. The normalized description allows lateral spread inside an upright-to-rounded dwarf framework.

### Lobe count

RHS gives five or seven; NC State and CBG give five to nine. The record preserves the range and flags possible species-level inheritance in structured fields.

### Flower colour

RHS says green; archived CBG says reddish-purple. No resolution is imposed. Flowers remain weak diagnostically.

### Autumn colour

Sources converge on yellow/gold/orange/red but differ in emphasis; CBG adds purple. The governed core uses gold/orange/scarlet/red, with purple as a lower-confidence possibility.

### Heat tolerance versus damage risk

NC State's relative heat-tolerance claim is qualified by both NC State and RHS warnings about temperature extremes, drought, wind and scorch. It must not be converted into heat immunity or drought tolerance.

### Seed production versus continuity

NC State reports self-seeding and weediness. The Atlas separates biological seed production from cultivar continuity: seedlings are not ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’ merely because the parent carried that label.

### Historical origin

Commercial Tōkai/Mikawa-region and 1970s discovery stories were located but excluded because no primary or authoritative traceable origin source was recovered.

## 4. Rejected-claim register

1. Hyphenated, spaced and title-case forms are separate cultivars.
2. The NC State name meaning is proven original etymology.
3. Commercial Tōkai/Mikawa wild-origin or 1970s discovery claims are established history.
4. “Dwarf” establishes a permanent 4-ft or 1.2-m maximum.
5. One layered branch authenticates the cultivar.
6. Layering alone separates it from every other dwarf maple.
7. It is identical to RC-008 ‘Shishi-gashira’.
8. Every leaf has one fixed lobe count or overlap angle.
9. Flowers are universally green or universally reddish-purple.
10. Relative heat tolerance means heat immunity, drought tolerance or scorch resistance.
11. Autumn foliage always turns one exact colour.
12. Bonsai-trained form represents untrained architecture.
13. Self-seeding produces additional plants of the named cultivar.
14. Seedlings inherit the cultivar identity.
15. Institutional labels or images prove universal clonal identity.
16. Online institutional images are automatically licensed for Atlas use.

## 5. G2 assessment

**PASS for G4 review.**

All canonical evidence domains have institutional or specialist sources and exact locators. The principal conflicts are explicit and manageable in a conservative draft. G4 remains conditional on the architecture/leaf-arrangement synthesis, RC-008 comparison, flower-colour conflict, heat/seedling treatment and acceptance of unresolved provenance.
