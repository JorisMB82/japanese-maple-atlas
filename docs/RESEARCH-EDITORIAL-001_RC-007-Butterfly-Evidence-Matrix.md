## 4. Principal conflict and uncertainty register

### 4.1 Accepted name and synonym sequence

**Evidence:** RHS directly links ‘Butterfly’ and ‘Kochō-no-mai’.  
**Unresolved:** which name was original, who assigned it, original characters, earliest catalogue and introduction sequence.  
**Governed treatment:** accept ‘Butterfly’ as working name and preserve ‘Kochō-no-mai’ as the RHS-recorded synonym without inventing history.

### 4.2 ‘White Butterfly’ confusion

**Evidence:** RHS maintains ‘White Butterfly’ as a separately accepted cultivar.  
**Governed treatment:** explicit non-equivalence; no synonym relationship.  
**Future evidence:** authenticated comparative description or registration history could strengthen diagnostic separation.

### 4.3 Size

- RHS: 2.5–4 m × 1–1.5 m, 10–20 years.
- Missouri Botanical Garden: 7–12 ft × 4–8 ft.
- UConn: approximately 15 ft with age, slow growing.

**Governed treatment:** preserve source frameworks and state that the cultivar is generally narrower than tall; do not publish one maximum or annual increment.

### 4.4 Autumn colour

**Evidence:** RHS retains cream/green/variegated in its autumn table; Missouri Botanical Garden flags good fall foliage but does not provide a detailed accessible colour description.  
**Governed treatment:** do not claim a fixed pink/red/orange autumn trajectory. Mark as an authenticated-observation priority.

### 4.5 Reversion

**Evidence:** no reviewed institutional source quantifies cultivar-specific reversion frequency.  
**Governed treatment:** describe non-variegated shoots as a management and identity question, not as a guaranteed event or separate cultivar.  
**Future evidence:** institutional cultivation records, specialist text with page locator or repeated accession observations.

### 4.6 Historical claims

Commercial pages report early dates and named distributors, but no primary catalogue or authoritative page-level specialist source was recovered. These statements are excluded from the approved historical core.

## 5. Rejected-claim register

1. ‘White Butterfly’ is a synonym of ‘Butterfly’.
2. ‘Kocho Nishiki’ is an approved synonym.
3. A stated nineteenth-century origin or 1938 distribution claim is proven without a primary locator.
4. One white-margined leaf identifies the cultivar.
5. Pink tinting alone authenticates the clone.
6. Every labelled ‘Kochō-no-mai’ and ‘Butterfly’ plant has a documented common clonal chain.
7. Every leaf has the same margin width.
8. Reversion occurs at one predictable rate.
9. A green shoot is automatically a separate cultivar.
10. Autumn foliage always follows one exact colour sequence.
11. One fixed mature size applies in all regions.
12. Full sun cannot cause scorch.
13. Herbarium labels or photograph captions prove universal clonal authenticity.
14. Seedlings inherit the cultivar name.
15. Online institutional images are automatically licensed for Atlas publication.
