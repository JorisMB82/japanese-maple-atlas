## 4. Evidence and locator summary

### Identity and naming

- Accepted Atlas form: ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’, following RHS and the G0 decision.
- NC State: ‘Mikawa yatsubusa’.
- JC Raulston/Chicago Botanic Garden: ‘Mikawa Yatsubusa’.
- These are search/display variants, not separate cultivar identities.
- No cultivar synonym is approved.
- NC State's “small cluster of three rivers” meaning is retained as a source-reported interpretation only.
- Commercial Tōkai/Mikawa-region and 1970s discovery stories are excluded pending primary evidence.

### Morphology and architecture

- Compact, slow-growing, dense and multibranched dwarf framework.
- RHS spreading-branched; Chicago upright; NC State multistemmed/dense.
- Tightly overlapping leaves create bunched, layered, tufted or small-canopy effects.
- RHS reports five or seven lobes; NC State/Chicago report five to nine.
- The detailed North American leaf fields are qualified because they may partly inherit species-level traits.

### Seasonal expression

- Spring: pale/yellow-green; Chicago reports early emergence.
- Summer: medium to bright green.
- Autumn: yellow-gold, orange, scarlet and red, with red edging reported by NC State; purple remains a lower-confidence archived possibility.
- RHS flower colour is green; Chicago reports reddish-purple. The conflict is preserved.
- Red-flushed/rich-red samaras are supported.
- Green bark/stems are reported, without a unique authentication claim.

### Dimensions

- RHS: 1–1.5 m high and wide; narrative about 1–1.2 m; 5–10 years.
- NC State: 4–6 ft high and 4 ft wide.
- Chicago archived profile: 5 ft in 10 years.
- JC Raulston general size: 3–4 ft, but accession 030154 reached 6 ft in 2022 after receipt in 2003 and planting in 2004.

The documented accession demonstrates that “dwarf” must not be converted into a fixed 4-ft maximum.

### Cultivation

- Full sun to partial shade; JC Raulston lists light shade.
- Reliable moisture and shelter are important.
- Moisture-retentive but well-drained soil is supported.
- RHS permits a broad pH range; NC State specifies acid to neutral. No universal alkaline-tolerance claim is approved.
- NC State reports relative heat tolerance but also temperature, wind, drought and underwatering damage; RHS warns about scorch.
- RHS H5, NC State zones 5b–9b and archived Chicago zones 6–9 remain separate source frameworks.
- Containers and bonsai are supported uses, not identity evidence.

### History and continuity

- JC Raulston accession received 22 January 2003, planted 17 May 2004, measured 6 ft on 8 June 2022.
- JCRA has twenty-four photographs dated from April 2005 through March 2026.
- Maple Society North America records it as 2015 Maple of the Year.
- Original selector, origin, date, earliest catalogue and primary name history remain unresolved.
- RHS publishes layering, grafting and softwood cuttings.
- NC State self-seeding language does not confer cultivar identity on seedlings.

## 7. Principal rejected claims

- spelling variants are separate cultivars;
- the name meaning is proven original etymology;
- commercial origin stories are established history;
- dwarf status establishes a permanent 4-ft or 1.2-m ceiling;
- one layered branch authenticates the cultivar;
- RC-010 is identical to RC-008;
- every leaf has one fixed lobe count or overlap angle;
- flowers are universally green or universally reddish-purple;
- relative heat tolerance means heat immunity or drought tolerance;
- full sun cannot cause scorch;
- autumn foliage has one exact colour;
- bonsai training represents natural architecture;
- self-seeding creates more plants of the cultivar;
- seedlings inherit the name;
- institutional labels or images prove universal clonal identity;
- online images are automatically reusable.
