## 4. Evidence and locator summary

### Identity and naming

- Institutional consensus supports *Acer palmatum* ‘Butterfly’ as the working name.
- RHS records *Acer palmatum* ‘Kochō-no-mai’ as a synonym and maintains a related accepted-name page.
- Macron, hyphen and spacing variations are treated as search/orthographic variants rather than separate cultivar entities.
- RHS maintains *Acer palmatum* ‘White Butterfly’ as a separately accepted cultivar; it is explicitly excluded from synonymy.
- ‘Kocho Nishiki’ and early-date commercial claims remain outside the approved evidence core.

### Morphology and architecture

- RHS supports a rather upright shrub or small tree with bushy habit.
- Leaves are five- to seven-lobed in the RHS cultivar description.
- RHS and UConn converge on green to grey-green centres with irregular cream-white margins and pink tinting.
- Irregularity is descriptive; one fixed margin width is not approved.

### Seasonal expression

- Spring: cream-white marginal variegation with frequent pink tinting; small red to reddish-purple flowers.
- Summer: green to grey-green centres with persistent cream-white margins.
- Autumn: institutional evidence is insufficient for one exact cultivar-specific trajectory; the cautious formulation is deliberate.
- No cultivar-specific winter bark diagnostic is approved.

### Dimensions

- RHS: 2.5–4 m high, 1–1.5 m spread, 10–20 years.
- Missouri Botanical Garden: 7–12 ft high, 4–8 ft spread.
- UConn: approximately 15 ft with age and slow growth.

The draft preserves the three source frameworks and does not create a universal maximum or growth increment.

### Cultivation

- Partial shade and sheltered conditions are preferred in RHS guidance.
- Full sun is conditional on regional heat and reliable moisture.
- RHS and Missouri Botanical Garden warn about scorch under excessive exposure, heat or moisture deficit.
- Moist, well-drained, organically rich, acid to neutral/slightly acidic soil is supported.
- RHS H6 and Missouri Botanical Garden USDA zones 5–8 remain separate frameworks.
- Grafting and softwood cuttings are published propagation methods.

### History and continuity

- RHS herbarium records material under the name on 14 April, 6 June and 18 October 2000.
- JC Raulston records eleven photographs dated from 2001 through 2014.
- These establish modern documented use, not original provenance or universal clonal authentication.
- Original selector, date, location, early catalogue and naming sequence remain unresolved.
- Cultivar-specific reversion frequency is not quantified in the reviewed institutional evidence.

## 7. Principal rejected claims

The draft rejects claims that:

- ‘White Butterfly’ is a synonym of ‘Butterfly’;
- ‘Kocho Nishiki’ is already an approved synonym;
- commercial 1882 or 1938 claims constitute established history without a primary locator;
- one white-margined leaf or pink tinting authenticates the cultivar;
- all leaves have an identical margin width;
- reversion occurs at one predictable frequency;
- a green shoot is automatically a separate cultivar;
- one exact autumn-colour sequence is guaranteed;
- the cultivar has one fixed mature maximum or annual increment;
- full sun cannot cause scorch;
- institutional labels or photograph captions prove universal clonal authenticity;
- seedlings inherit the cultivar identity;
- online institutional images are automatically reusable.
