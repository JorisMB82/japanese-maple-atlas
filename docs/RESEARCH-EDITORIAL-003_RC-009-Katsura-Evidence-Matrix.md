## 3. Principal conflict and uncertainty register

### Lobe count

RHS reports five to seven lobes; Missouri's detailed cultivar paragraph reports five. Both are retained. The record rejects a single universal count.

### Spring colour terminology

RHS uses yellow-orange with pink margins; Missouri uses chartreuse with orange margins. The governed range preserves both. No fixed digital colour or guaranteed margin width is approved.

### Dimensions and age

RHS presents 2.5–4 m dimensions over 20–50 years. Missouri reports 4–6 ft at about ten years and 10–12 ft ultimately. These are compatible contextual frameworks but do not establish one growth curve.

### Autumn expression

RHS describes bright orange; Missouri describes pastel orange and yellow. The governed range is orange to yellow, with bright-to-pastel variation.

### Cross-taxon confusion

The common word “Katsura” also denotes *Cercidiphyllum japonicum*. Evidence about that species is prohibited from entering this cultivar record unless explicitly used for disambiguation.

### Provenance

Institutional records establish modern use from at least 1995, not original selection or introduction. No primary naming statement, selector, date or earliest catalogue was recovered.

## 4. Rejected-claim register

1. *Acer palmatum* ‘Katsura’ is taxonomically related to *Cercidiphyllum japonicum* because of shared wording.
2. History or cultivation of the katsura tree applies to the cultivar.
3. One yellow-orange spring leaf authenticates ‘Katsura’.
4. Orange/pink spring edging is permanent cream-white variegation equivalent to RC-007 ‘Butterfly’.
5. The cultivar remains yellow throughout summer.
6. Early leaf-out guarantees frost damage every year.
7. Autumn foliage is always one exact orange.
8. One universal mature size or annual increment applies.
9. Full sun cannot cause scorch.
10. Herbarium labels or photograph captions prove universal clonal identity.
11. Seedlings inherit the cultivar name.
12. Online institutional images are automatically licensed for Atlas use.
13. A translation, naming rationale or place of origin can be inferred without primary evidence.
