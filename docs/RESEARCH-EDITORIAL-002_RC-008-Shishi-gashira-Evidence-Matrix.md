## 3. Principal conflict and uncertainty register

### Spelling and synonyms

- RHS accepted working form: ‘Shishi-gashira’.
- Missouri Botanical Garden, JC Raulston and RHS specimen labels: ‘Shishigashira’.
- RHS synonym set: ‘Ribesifolium’, ‘Crispifolium’, ‘Mejishi’.
- RHS descriptive typo: “Shish geshira”.

**Governed treatment:** hyphenated Atlas working form; unhyphenated search variant; preserve institutional synonyms; reject typo.

### Architecture versus leaf arrangement

The “lion’s mane” effect combines upright compact branching, close internodes and small crinkled leaves held in tufts. A leaf-only description is incomplete, while “whorled” would be botanically stronger than the evidence supports.

### Size

RHS, Missouri and JC Raulston provide differing frameworks. The record preserves all and rejects a universal maximum or dwarf classification.

### Autumn colour

RHS says soft orange; Missouri says gold and crimson. These are compatible stages or environmental expressions. The governed range retains all three colours.

### Comparison with RC-010

‘Shishi-gashira’ is expected to be taller and defined by tufted/crinkled texture; ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’ is expected to be more dwarf and defined by layered overlap. This distinction remains provisional until RC-010 evidence is assembled.

### Historical provenance

The 1982 specimen establishes modern institutional use by that date but not selection or introduction history. No primary Japanese catalogue or original naming statement was located.

## 4. Rejected-claim register

1. “Shish geshira” is an accepted name.
2. Hyphenated and unhyphenated forms are separate cultivars.
3. All RHS synonyms have proven identical historical priority and scope.
4. “Lion’s head” proves place, date or selector.
5. One curled leaf authenticates the cultivar.
6. Tufted presentation proves botanical whorling.
7. Every young plant is densely foliated.
8. The cultivar is a true dwarf equivalent to ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’.
9. Autumn foliage is always only orange or always only crimson.
10. One fixed mature size or annual increment applies universally.
11. Full sun cannot cause scorch.
12. Early emergence guarantees frost damage annually.
13. Institutional labels or photographs prove universal clonal identity.
14. Seedlings inherit the cultivar name.
15. Online institutional images are automatically licensed for Atlas use.
