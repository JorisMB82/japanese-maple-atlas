# CATALOGUE-COMPARISONS-001 — High-Confusion Visual Clusters

**Status:** ACTIVE EDITORIAL GUIDANCE — not a photographic authentication key  
**Inspected `main`:** `f974b0d8bda95c4dc8f3ef1013853de498b2cdd7`  
**Scope:** practical caption and media-review controls for CUL-000011 through CUL-000025 and the closest Reference Standard comparisons.

## 1. Use rule

Visible traits support comparison, not clonal authentication. A source-asserted photograph may illustrate source-labelled habit, foliage and seasonal expression when the item does not contradict the profile. It cannot, by itself, establish the accepted name, taxon, synonymy, origin, propagation history or exclusive diagnostic identity.

Use the standard disclosure for non-documented items:

> The source or contributor identifies this plant as the named cultivar. The Japanese Maple Atlas has not independently authenticated the clone. Corrections and corroborating photographs are welcome.

## 2. Red dissectums

**Cultivars:** ‘Inaba-shidare’, ‘Crimson Queen’, ‘Tamukeyama’, ‘Red Dragon’ and ‘Orangeola’.

### Useful visible distinctions

- **‘Inaba-shidare’:** relatively vigorous, arching and cascading; large purple-red dissected leaves; often broader and more substantial than compact forms.
- **‘Crimson Queen’:** compact, low and cascading Reference Standard comparison; use the governed RC description rather than generic red-laceleaf assumptions.
- **‘Tamukeyama’:** broad mounding and cascading habit; burgundy-purple spring foliage, greener-purple summer and orange-to-red autumn.
- **‘Red Dragon’:** compact cascading habit with strong burgundy-red retention in suitable conditions.
- **‘Orangeola’:** orange-red emergence and later flushes, reddish-green summer foliage and renewed orange-red autumn colour.

### Claims requiring qualification

Comparative vigour, sun tolerance, colour retention, exact mature dimensions and the exclusivity of any leaf shape must remain qualified. Training height, rootstock, climate, moisture and age can change apparent habit and colour.

### What a source-asserted image may support

It may support that a named source presented a plant as the cultivar and that the photographed plant shows a stated habit, attached foliage or seasonal phase compatible with the profile.

### What it cannot support

It cannot prove that an unlabeled red dissectum is the named clone, resolve synonymy, exclude all other red dissectums, or establish that one colour phase is stable across climates.

## 3. Narrow and linearilobum forms

**Cultivars:** ‘Koto-no-ito’ and ‘Red Pygmy’.

### Useful visible distinctions

- **‘Koto-no-ito’:** green thread-like lobes; rounded semi-dwarf habit; orange-yellow autumn colour; early and later leaves may differ in width.
- **‘Red Pygmy’:** red-purple linearilobum foliage, usually with long narrow lobes; compact in the RHS framework but capable of greater scale in at least one documented accession.

### Claims requiring qualification

Lobe count, exact lobe width, mature size and early-versus-later leaf differences are not absolute. Near-name cultivars must be separated by governed identity, not typography or narrow foliage alone.

### What a source-asserted image may support

It may illustrate green versus red-purple foliage, general narrow-lobed form, attached-leaf context and the photographed plant’s scale.

### What it cannot support

It cannot establish that every narrow-lobed green plant is ‘Koto-no-ito’, every red narrow-lobed plant is ‘Red Pygmy’, or that a single measured specimen defines the cultivar’s universal size.

## 4. Bark- and stem-focused cultivars

**Cultivars:** ‘Beni-kawa’, ‘Aoyagi’ and ‘Arakawa’. Closest Reference Standard comparison: ‘Sango-kaku’.

### Useful visible distinctions

- **‘Beni-kawa’:** coral-red young stems, green summer leaves and yellow-gold autumn colour; generally treated within a smaller published framework than some coral-bark comparisons.
- **‘Aoyagi’:** pea-green to bright-green young bark with green foliage and yellow autumn interest.
- **‘Arakawa’:** corky rough bark developing on older trunk and branches; ordinary green palmate foliage; landscape habit must be distinguished from bonsai training.
- **‘Sango-kaku’:** separate governed Reference Standard identity; coral-bark resemblance does not create synonymy.

### Claims requiring qualification

Stem colour changes with age, exposure and season. Rough bark develops over time. Bonsai form is training, not ordinary cultivar habit. English labels such as “rough bark maple” can be descriptive rather than unique names.

### What a source-asserted image may support

It may illustrate the source-labelled specimen’s stem colour, age-related bark texture, attached foliage and whole-plant context.

### What it cannot support

It cannot authenticate a cultivar from bark colour alone, prove that a seedling or `ex` plant preserves the clone, or substitute a bonsai silhouette for normal landscape architecture.

## 5. Variegated or seasonally unstable-looking foliage

**Cultivars:** ‘Butterfly’ and ‘Higasa-yama’.

### Useful visible distinctions

- **‘Butterfly’:** governed Reference Standard comparison with small variegated leaves and its own accepted identity and uncertainty record.
- **‘Higasa-yama’:** upright habit; crinkled spring leaves with pale or pink-white variegation; foliage may become greener later in the season.

### Claims requiring qualification

Variegation intensity, distribution and persistence vary by shoot, season, exposure and specimen. Reversion, scorch and photography can alter apparent pattern. `Higasa-yama`, `Higasa yama` and `Higasayama` are spelling forms of one identity, not visual diagnoses.

### What a source-asserted image may support

It may show a source-labelled plant’s current variegation, attached leaves, branch context and seasonal change when dates are supplied.

### What it cannot support

It cannot prove stable year-round variegation, distinguish every variegated cultivar from one leaf, or authenticate a clone from label spelling.

## 6. Golden, yellow and orange spring types

**Cultivars:** ‘Orange Dream’ and ‘Autumn Moon’. Closest comparisons: ‘Katsura’ and *Acer shirasawanum* ‘Aureum’.

### Useful visible distinctions

- **‘Orange Dream’:** *Acer palmatum*; yellow to yellow-orange spring foliage, possible orange-red margin, greener summer foliage and orange-yellow autumn colour.
- **‘Autumn Moon’:** *Acer shirasawanum*; rounded many-lobed leaves with yellow-green and burnt-orange or salmon spring tones, greener or yellow-veined summer development and orange-red autumn colour.
- **‘Katsura’:** separate *Acer palmatum* Reference Standard comparison with its own chartreuse and yellow-orange spring trajectory.
- **‘Aureum’:** separate *Acer shirasawanum* Reference Standard identity; yellow foliage similarity does not imply equivalence.

### Claims requiring qualification

Exact hue, margin colour, persistence, lobe count and timing depend on light, climate and observation date. Older commercial labels placing ‘Autumn Moon’ under *Acer japonicum* or *Acer palmatum* do not create separate clones.

### What a source-asserted image may support

It may illustrate attached foliage, whole-plant habit and the source-labelled seasonal phase when the image is compatible with the accepted profile.

### What it cannot support

It cannot authenticate a yellow-leaved plant from colour alone, resolve taxon history, prove a fixed lobe count or establish that one exposure represents the cultivar everywhere.

## 7. Caption construction rule

A useful public caption should normally contain:

1. the visible subject and visual role;
2. the source’s identification basis;
3. one or two profile-supported facts;
4. a qualification where colour, size, variegation, bark or comparison is contextual;
5. the item-level identity-confidence disclosure.

Avoid captions that say “this proves,” “always,” “uniquely identifiable,” “true form,” or “definitive” unless a separately governed evidentiary record supports the claim.
