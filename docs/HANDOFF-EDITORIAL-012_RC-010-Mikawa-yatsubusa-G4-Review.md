## Botanical and editorial determination

Institutional evidence supports a very compact, slow-growing, dense and multibranched dwarf shrub or small tree. Its principal diagnostic effect is produced by small whole-plant scale combined with tightly overlapping palmate leaves that form layered, tufted or miniature-canopy effects along branches.

RHS reports five or seven lobes, while NC State and the archived Chicago Botanic Garden profile report five to nine. The source difference is preserved. Published dimensions demonstrate that “dwarf” is relative rather than a fixed four-foot maximum: the JC Raulston accession was received in 2003, planted in 2004 and measured at six feet in 2022.

Descriptive confidence is high for compact architecture and layered foliage. A compact plant, isolated leaf, branch photograph or nursery label does not authenticate the clone.

## Naming and identity decisions

- ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’ remains the locked RHS-hyphenated working form.
- Spaced and title-case forms remain institutional/search variants.
- No cultivar synonym is approved from the reviewed institutional set.
- NC State’s name meaning is retained only as a source-reported interpretation.
- Commercial Tōkai/Mikawa-region and 1970s discovery narratives remain excluded pending primary evidence.

## Source-sidecar review

The archived Chicago profile remains explicitly archival. Its reddish-purple flower account conflicts with the RHS green-flower account; the conflict is preserved rather than normalized. NC State’s relative heat-tolerance language remains qualified by its temperature, drought, wind and scorch cautions. Source-reported self-seeding does not preserve cultivar identity.

## Rejected claims retained

Orthographic variants do not create separate cultivars; source-reported etymology is not proven original meaning; “dwarf” is not a fixed height ceiling; all flowers do not have one proven colour; relative heat tolerance is not immunity to scorch; seedlings do not inherit cultivar identity; Maple of the Year recognition does not prove origin or clonal authenticity.

## Comparison review

RC-008 remains the essential close comparator: ‘Shishi-gashira’ emphasizes crinkled leaves and close tufting on a taller dense framework, while RC-010 emphasizes dwarf scale and overlapping layered foliage. RC-004 and RC-005 provide compact-use and seasonal/taxonomic contrasts.
