## 4. Evidence and locator summary

### Identity and naming

- Accepted working name: *Acer palmatum* ‘Katsura’.
- No cultivar synonym is approved from the reviewed institutional source set.
- The epithet must be distinguished from *Cercidiphyllum japonicum*, commonly called katsura tree.
- No naming translation, geographic origin or historical rationale is inferred without primary evidence.

### Morphology and architecture

- Dense, compact and upright to vase-shaped when young.
- Crown becomes more rounded with age in the Missouri account.
- Small palmate leaves.
- Five lobes in Missouri; five to seven in RHS.
- Missouri reports lanceolate lobes, slender tips and shallow-toothed margins.

### Seasonal expression

- Spring: chartreuse to yellow-orange with orange or pink margins.
- Summer: bright green.
- Autumn: bright or pastel orange and yellow.
- The spring window is the principal descriptive and product value.
- Early leaf-out is reported and carries late-frost risk.

### Dimensions

- RHS: 2.5–4 m high and wide, with a 20–50-year maturity framework.
- Missouri: 4–6 ft after approximately ten years, ultimately 10–12 ft high and wide.

No universal mature maximum, crown ratio or annual increment is approved.

### Cultivation

- Full sun to partial shade, with hot-climate partial-shade qualification.
- Moist, organically rich, well-drained, acid to neutral/slightly acidic soil.
- High-pH chlorosis risk.
- Heat/exposure/moisture-deficit scorch risk.
- Early-leaf-out and late-frost risk.
- RHS H6 and Missouri USDA zones 5–9 remain separate frameworks.
- RHS publishes grafting and softwood cuttings.

### History

- RHS herbarium records material dated 15 July 1995, June 1997 and 6 June 2000.
- JC Raulston records four photographs dated 1 May 2018.
- Original selector, place, date, earliest catalogue and naming rationale remain unresolved.
