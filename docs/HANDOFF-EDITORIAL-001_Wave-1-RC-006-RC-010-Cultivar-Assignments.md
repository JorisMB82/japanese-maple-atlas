## 3. Structured assignment register

### RC-006 — *Acer palmatum* ‘Osakazuki’

- **Principal reason for inclusion:** Well-documented, horticulturally important green-leaved cultivar whose strongest ornamental expression is autumn colour. It is suitable as the first live canonical RC because it provides broad institutional evidence while retaining real synonym, classification and provenance questions.
- **Diagnostic/product role within RC-001–RC-010:** Establishes the green-summer / brilliant-autumn-red profile and tests whether the Atlas distinguishes seasonal description from cultivar authentication.
- **Likely comparison relationships:**
  - RC-001 ‘Bloodgood’ — substantial habit and red autumn overlap, but contrasting summer foliage.
  - RC-003 ‘Sango-kaku’ — green foliage and upright/rounded architecture, but bark-led versus autumn-foliage-led value.
  - RC-005 ‘Aureum’ — green versus yellow growing-season expression and different species placement.
  - RC-009 ‘Katsura’ — autumn-led versus spring-emergence-led seasonal identity.
- **Known naming or identity risks:** RHS display form ‘Ōsakazuki’; source-recorded ‘Taihai’ synonym; source-specific Amoenum/Elegans/Heptalobum formulations; unresolved JC Raulston spelling ‘Ōsakasuki’; original selector, date and primary Japanese naming evidence unresolved.
- **Source availability:** **Strong.** Dedicated RHS, Missouri Botanical Garden and NC State profiles; Kew species context; RHS herbarium records; JC Raulston living-accession data.
- **Anticipated media difficulty:** **Moderate.** Autumn material is visually abundant, but a primary asset must show documented specimen identity, whole-plant habit and broad palmate foliage rather than generic red autumn colour.
- **Duplicate/conflict check:** No duplicate with RC-001–RC-005. Some product overlap with RC-001 is intentional and comparison-positive. No conflict with the other four Wave 1 assignments.
- **Recommendation:** **PASS.** Preserve the existing RC-006 research package after baseline and media-ownership corrections.

### RC-007 — *Acer palmatum* ‘Butterfly’

- **Principal reason for inclusion:** Introduces stable cultivar variegation, irregular cream-white margins, pink seasonal tinting and the editorial problem of reversion or non-uniform shoots.
- **Diagnostic/product role within RC-001–RC-010:** First variegated Reference Standard; tests whether the product can represent within-plant variability without confusing it with identity uncertainty.
- **Likely comparison relationships:**
  - RC-005 ‘Aureum’ — yellow foliage versus white-margined variegation.
  - RC-009 ‘Katsura’ — seasonal colour change versus persistent variegated pattern.
  - RC-003 ‘Sango-kaku’ — upright small-tree architecture with a different principal ornamental domain.
- **Known naming or identity risks:** RHS records ‘Kochō-no-mai’ as a synonym. Variegation can vary by shoot, season and exposure; reverted green shoots must not be represented as a separate cultivar without evidence. Commercial material may be labelled by English or Japanese-associated names.
- **Source availability:** **Strong to moderate.** Dedicated RHS and Missouri Botanical Garden profiles; RHS herbarium records; JC Raulston institutional photograph collection. Primary historical origin evidence is expected to be weaker than descriptive evidence.
- **Anticipated media difficulty:** **Moderate–high.** Requires whole-plant habit, representative variegated leaf detail, seasonal colour and explicit treatment of reversion. Overexposure can erase white margins in photographs.
- **Duplicate/conflict check:** No duplicate with current records. Its variegation role is unique in the first ten. No material conflict with RC-008–RC-010.
- **Recommendation:** **PASS.** Naming and reversion treatment are G1–G4 research requirements, not assignment blockers.

### RC-008 — *Acer palmatum* ‘Shishi-gashira’

- **Principal reason for inclusion:** Adds a distinctive dense upright or bushy architecture with masses of small, crowded, curled or crinkled leaves and strong autumn transformation.
- **Diagnostic/product role within RC-001–RC-010:** Tests dense architectural texture and the distinction between whole-plant habit, internode/leaf crowding and individual leaf morphology.
- **Likely comparison relationships:**
  - RC-003 ‘Sango-kaku’ — upright habit but different bark and foliage diagnostics.
  - RC-010 ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’ — deliberately close comparison: taller lion’s-mane-type density versus dwarf layered/overlapping foliage.
  - RC-002 ‘Seiryu’ — fine visual texture produced by crowded small leaves versus deeply dissected leaves.
- **Known naming or identity risks:** RHS accepted spelling ‘Shishi-gashira’; JC Raulston and other sources use ‘Shishigashira’. RHS records ‘Ribesifolium’, ‘Crispifolium’ and ‘Mejishi’ as synonyms. The RHS descriptive text itself contains a spelling error (‘Shish geshira’), which must not be propagated.
- **Source availability:** **Strong.** Detailed RHS profile and synonyms; multiple RHS herbarium records including a 1982 specimen; JC Raulston living accession and extensive seasonal photograph collection; Missouri Botanical Garden listing.
- **Anticipated media difficulty:** **Moderate.** The cultivar is visually distinctive, but the package needs both whole-plant architecture and close detail of crowded/curled foliage. A tight leaf crop alone could obscure the lion’s-mane effect.
- **Duplicate/conflict check:** Not a duplicate of RC-010. The two are retained as an instructive comparison pair and must be visually and textually differentiated. No conflict with RC-001–RC-005.
- **Recommendation:** **PASS.** Synonym and spelling normalization must be resolved before G3.

### RC-009 — *Acer palmatum* ‘Katsura’

- **Principal reason for inclusion:** Introduces a cultivar defined primarily by early spring emergence: chartreuse or yellow-orange leaves with orange/pink margins, maturing green and later returning to orange/yellow.
- **Diagnostic/product role within RC-001–RC-010:** First spring-emergence-led record; tests temporal search and comparison across spring, summer and autumn rather than a single dominant colour.
- **Likely comparison relationships:**
  - RC-006 ‘Osakazuki’ — spring-led versus autumn-led seasonal value.
  - RC-005 ‘Aureum’ — transient spring yellow-orange versus sustained yellow/yellow-green foliage under a different species.
  - RC-007 ‘Butterfly’ — seasonal marginal colour versus structural variegation.
- **Known naming or identity risks:** The cultivar epithet ‘Katsura’ can be confused in general search with the unrelated katsura tree, *Cercidiphyllum japonicum*. Early leaf-out and late-frost injury can materially alter the expected spring display. Exact origin and naming history require primary-source work.
- **Source availability:** **Strong.** Detailed RHS profile; Missouri Botanical Garden dedicated Plant Finder profile and Plant of Merit treatment; additional institutional collection leads.
- **Anticipated media difficulty:** **Moderate–high.** The defining spring window is brief. Same-accession spring, summer and autumn views are strongly preferred; a summer-only image would miss the cultivar’s principal product value.
- **Duplicate/conflict check:** Not a duplicate of RC-005 or RC-006 because its defining trait is the spring trajectory, not persistent yellow foliage or autumn red. No conflict with RC-007/RC-008.
- **Recommendation:** **PASS.** Media acquisition should begin early enough to capture bud break and first-leaf colour.

### RC-010 — *Acer palmatum* ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’

- **Principal reason for inclusion:** Adds a true compact/dwarf product case with dense, tightly overlapping foliage producing a layered or stacked effect and a distinctive small-space/bonsai use profile.
- **Diagnostic/product role within RC-001–RC-010:** Completes the first-ten morphology portfolio with dwarf scale and layered foliage. It is an appropriate RC-010 formative-checkpoint record because it tests whether the canonical profile handles close architecture/morphology interaction without conflating the two.
- **Likely comparison relationships:**
  - RC-008 ‘Shishi-gashira’ — dense foliage comparison, but materially different scale, layering and crown development.
  - RC-004 ‘Crimson Queen’ — compact garden use but upright/layered green foliage versus cascading red dissectum foliage.
  - RC-005 ‘Aureum’ — small-space ornamental use and spring yellow-green expression, but distinct leaf structure and species.
- **Known naming or identity risks:** RHS uses ‘Mikawa-yatsubusa’; NC State uses ‘Mikawa yatsubusa’; JC Raulston uses ‘Mikawa Yatsubusa’. Source-reported name meanings require primary Japanese verification. Published size frameworks diverge, and living-accession dimensions may exceed simplified dwarf marketing descriptions.
- **Source availability:** **Strong.** Dedicated RHS profile; dedicated NC State Extension profile; JC Raulston living accession with received, planted and measured dates; institutional image leads.
- **Anticipated media difficulty:** **Moderate.** Layering is highly visual, but scale must be shown accurately. Whole-plant, branch architecture and overlapping-leaf details are required; isolated leaves alone are insufficient.
- **Duplicate/conflict check:** Deliberately paired, not duplicated, with RC-008. The editorial and media packages must state the distinctions clearly. No conflict with RC-001–RC-005.
- **Recommendation:** **PASS.** Replaces the earlier *Acer japonicum* ‘Aconitifolium’ proposal to avoid an unapproved Wave 1 taxon dependency.
